# Style guide

## Mark-making

Think of a small ink drawing reproduced in print. A readable contour and a few solid dark masses do the structural work. Short hatching follows the surface; stipple density supplies intermediate values. Preserve clean paper highlights within the subject. Avoid a glossy 3D material, airbrushed shading, a pixel-art grid, or uniformly distressed edges unless the user requests a hybrid.

The bundled image demonstrates the ink treatment, not a universal character, scene, breed, or camera angle. Its background is deliberately dense; reduce this density for small assets or text-adjacent artwork. Do not copy anatomy defects from any style reference.

## Composition by destination

| Destination | Adaptation |
| --- | --- |
| Hero or editorial scene | One clear focal subject; separate foreground, middle distance, and background through ink density and scale. Leave the required text-safe region quiet. |
| Small spot illustration | Fewer internal marks, stronger silhouette, simpler objects; check at intended size instead of forcing scenic detail. |
| Transparent cutout | Retain opaque highlights belonging to the subject. Remove only the backdrop; do not draw a checkerboard or paper rectangle. |
| Series | Consistent camera height, visual size, margins, contour weight, dot size, and light direction. |

## Character and action

Identity consists of proportions, silhouette, markings, distinctive details, and material. A pose change should not quietly alter those. Describe an action using supports, weight, joint bends, and gaze direction. Determine which limbs bear weight and which are moving; every limb need not touch a surface. Check the supports that the requested action actually needs. A head direction, a facial view, and a camera direction are separate choices.

For natural fur or material transitions, use directional strokes and interleaved patches rather than a smooth gradient band. Do not make every subject cute, humanlike, or feline; that is a brief-level decision.

## Color and theme

Monochrome is the default style grammar. Warm paper is optional, not a mandatory background. When an accent is requested, use the supplied color consistently and preserve requested monochrome objects. A dark page can use the original illustration on its paper matte, or a separately art-directed dark background. Automatic full-image inversion changes identity colors and should not be treated as a default theme solution.
